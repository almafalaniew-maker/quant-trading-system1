# 2020–2026: maximising money, and why the biggest number is not the answer

## The trap

A full sweep of every lever on 2020–2026 returned the *same* configuration as
the winner in all seven stages — 8R target, 250-bar hold, 1.0% risk, 15 slots —
at **$995,332** from $100,000, with every neighbouring value falling to
$400–700k. A setting worth $400k more than its neighbours in every direction is
not an optimum. It is one lucky compounding path.

Two perturbation tests confirm it. Neither should matter much:

**Opening balance** (all normalised back to $100k):

| start | result |
| ---: | ---: |
| $95,000 | $551,401 |
| $98,000 | $988,807 |
| **$100,000** | **$995,332** |
| $101,000 | $548,388 |
| $105,000 | $540,405 |
| $110,000 | $690,222 |

Starting with $101,000 instead of $100,000 costs **45% of the profit**.

**Slippage**: 3bps → $696,920 · 4bps → $1,001,946 · 5bps → $995,332 · 7bps →
$681,490. One basis point moves $300,000.

The cause is mechanical: at 1% risk, cash and slot availability decide *which*
signals become trades. Nudge anything and the path reshuffles, you catch or miss
a different set of 8R winners, and compounding amplifies the difference.

Running every candidate across 11 opening balances turns one number into a
distribution. Ranked by **median** rather than best draw:

**Ranking by median changes the winner.** The configuration that topped every
single-path sweep finishes third, and is by far the least stable — a 1.46×
spread against 1.02× for the winner.

## What to actually run

**For most money: 8R target, 250-bar hold, 1.0% risk, 15 slots, gap ≥2.5%
required.** ~$777k median (35.9% a year), and it lands between $774k and $786k
regardless of path. The stability is the point: you get roughly this outcome
rather than a coin flip between $540k and $995k.

**For best ride: the same with a 1.5×ATR stop.** $748k median at a **17.6%**
drawdown instead of 25.7% — giving up 4% of the money for a third less pain, and
a 1.00× spread, the most stable result in the table.

**More size does not help.** 1.0% risk beat 1.5%, 2.0%, 2.5% and 3.0% in the
sweep. Larger positions consume cash and crowd out the very trades that produce
the 8R winners. Removing the regime gate: $340,992 at a 35.9% drawdown. Dropping
the trend filter: $529,978.

## A caution that matters

The gap filter wins here and **destroyed returns over 2006–2020** (cut them by a
third). Its value is not stable across eras, so the top line of this table is a
statement about 2020–2026, not about the strategy. Since a 6.7-year window with
one losing year cannot tell you which era you are in next, the 1.5×ATR-stop row
— which wins on stability and drawdown rather than on the gap — is the safer
reading of this table.

Every dollar figure still rests on a survivorship-selected universe. The
comparisons between rows are what this supports.
